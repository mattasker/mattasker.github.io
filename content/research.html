Research

I study evolution using mathematical models, ideas from statistical physics, and computer simulations.

Adaptation on fitness landscapes

A mutation’s effect depends on the genetic background in which it occurs. I study how these interactions shape adaptation, and how adaptation itself changes the effects of future mutations.

In my current work with Arne Traulsen, I investigate how selection reorganises the genetic contributions to fitness. Can populations with similar fitness respond differently to new mutations because of their evolutionary histories? What does this mean for their ability to adapt further?

More broadly, I’m interested in how mutation bias, interactions between genes, and competition between evolving lineages shape the pace and predictability of adaptation.

Evolution in changing environments

Environmental fluctuations change both the conditions populations experience and the strength of chance events. My work examines how these fluctuations interact with population size and spatial structure to influence coexistence, fixation and extinction.

With Mauro Mobilia and collaborators, I have studied these questions in models of microbial competition and cooperative antimicrobial resistance. I’m also investigating how migration in response to stress affects evolutionary outcomes.
